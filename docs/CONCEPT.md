# Innenkind – Concept

A guided session with the inner child in the browser: as close to a one-to-one
therapy session as a web page can responsibly be — and honest about where it
has to stop.

As of: 2026-09-28

---

## 1. What it is about

"Inner child" is not a diagnostic term but an image: the experiences, feelings
and conclusions we had as children are not over. They live on as a layer of
their own and switch on when a situation today resembles one from back then.
Then we react with the force and the means of back then — although we are adults
now and would have other options.

At its core, inner child work consists of four movements:

1. **Recognising** which early experiences and conclusions still have a say today
   (needs that went short; core beliefs; protective strategies).
2. **Meeting** — perceiving the child of back then inwardly, with the eyes and
   the means of the adult of today.
3. **Re-nurturing** — giving the child, in inner experience, what was missing
   back then (protection, comfort, being seen, permission). Schema therapy calls
   this *limited reparenting*, imagery work calls it *rescripting*.
4. **Bringing it into everyday life** — repeating the new inner relationship until
   it holds: small daily rituals, a plan for trigger situations, new believable
   sentences instead of the old ones.

## 2. Professional foundations

The site invents nothing of its own; it combines proven elements from several
schools. Every element is mapped to its origin in the code and in the interface.

| Source | What we take from it |
| --- | --- |
| **Transactional analysis** (Eric Berne, 1960s) | Ego states Parent / Adult / Child as the basic idea: we switch between inner states. |
| **John Bradshaw**, *Homecoming* (1990); **Charles Whitfield**, *Healing the Child Within* (1987) | Popularised inner child work, the letter to the child, developmental stages. |
| **Lucia Capacchione**, *Recovery of Your Inner Child* (1991) | The letter *from* the child, written with the non-dominant hand. |
| **Stefanie Stahl**, *Das Kind in dir muss Heimat finden* (2015) | Shadow child / sun child, core beliefs, protective strategies, the adult self comforting the shadow child. The best-known approach in German-speaking countries. |
| **Schema therapy** (Jeffrey Young; Arntz & Jacob) | The five core emotional needs, child modes (vulnerable, angry, happy), parent modes (demanding, punitive), the *healthy adult*, **imagery rescripting**. Well researched. |
| **Imagery rescripting** (Arntz & Weertman 1999; Morina et al. 2017, meta-analysis) | The central exercise: a distressing childhood scene is recalled and changed in imagination by an adult stepping in and meeting the child's need. |
| **Internal Family Systems** (Richard Schwartz) | Honouring protectors instead of fighting them; the question "How do you feel towards the child?" — whoever feels rejection is speaking from another part right now, not from the Self. |
| **PITT** (Luise Reddemann) | Stabilisation before confrontation: safe place, inner helpers, vault, observer technique (distance), resource-oriented. |
| **Compassion Focused Therapy** (Paul Gilbert); **self-compassion** (Kristin Neff) | Three emotion systems (threat, drive, soothing), the compassionate self, the self-compassion break. Good evidence for self-compassion interventions. |
| **Klaus Grawe**, consistency theory | The four psychological basic needs as a second grid for evaluating needs. |
| **Cognitive behavioural therapy** | Rating core beliefs on a 0–100 % scale, collecting evidence, *realistic* counter-statements instead of affirmations. |
| **Peter Gollwitzer**, implementation intentions | "If …, then …" plans for trigger situations. Solid evidence for turning intentions into action. |
| **Window of tolerance** (Dan Siegel), SUD scale (Wolpe) | Ask for distress on 0–10 before and after the imagery; if distress is too high, stabilise instead of going deeper. |

**To be honest:** "inner child work" as a method of its own has hardly been
studied in randomised trials. Several of its components are well supported,
though — imagery rescripting, schema therapy, self-compassion, implementation
intentions. Online self-help demonstrably works, but to a smaller extent than
guided therapy. The site says so too.

## 3. What the site should be — and what not

**Should:**

- a complete, guided session of about two hours that feels like good sessions
  with an experienced therapist: arriving, framing, tracing, mirroring, meeting,
  re-nurturing, closing, homework. After the mirroring there is a break point
  where it can be split over two appointments — like an initial consultation and
  a working session.
- **personal**: the answers produce an individual evaluation (needs, core
  beliefs, protective strategies), individual sentences for the child, a draft
  letter and an everyday plan.
- **responsive**: at the points where a therapist in a real session would respond
  to what is experienced ("The child looks away" — "You feel anger towards the
  child"), the site responds with text of its own.
- **a positive outcome**: every session ends stabilised, with something concrete
  to take away (letter, sentences, plan) and a visible before/after.
- **repeatable**: a daily short ritual, an exercise library, follow-up sessions.

**Should not:**

- replace therapy, diagnose or work through trauma. Severe trauma, dissociation
  and acute crises belong in professional care.
- lead people alone into being overwhelmed. Hence the safety architecture in
  section 6.
- collect data. Everything stays in the browser.

## 4. Structure of the session

The session follows the classic arc of a therapy session. Three paths differ in
depth and length. "Gentle" is not shorter but more careful: it only leaves out
the work on distressing memories — like the three questionnaire depths in
LinuxKompass. The opening conversation suggests a path, the person chooses, and
switching is possible at any time (towards "gentler" always, even in the middle
of the imagery).

| Path | Duration | For whom | What is missing compared with the deepest path |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Gentle** | approx. 115 min | High distress, known trauma or dissociation, first contact with inner work | No work on a distressing memory. The child is met at the safe place and from a distance. |
| **Encounter** | approx. 120 min | The usual case | No protector dialogue, no letter from the child, shorter tracing. |
| **Depth** | approx. 140 min | Those who bring time, calm and experience with imagery | – |

### Phase 0 – Opening conversation (all paths)

- How are you right now? (wellbeing 0–10, distress 0–10)
- Safety question: thoughts of not wanting to live any more. On "Yes, right now"
  the path ends here, kindly, at the crisis page — with numbers, a calming
  exercise and no memory work.
- Being overwhelmed / drifting away / blackouts with memories → recommend "Gentle".
- Ongoing psychotherapy → a note to discuss the session with the therapist and
  bring the results along.
- Time: one hour (then split), two hours, more. The time estimates are honestly
  calculated — a first version saying "40 to 90 minutes" did not survive
  recalculation.
- Time, place, being undisturbed, experience with imagery → suggested path.

### Phase 1 – Arriving

- Frame: what happens, how long, that everything is voluntary, that the pause
  button is always there, that tears, tiredness or "feeling nothing" are normal.
- Concern: what brings you here? (relationship, self-worth, anger, fear,
  emptiness, curiosity …) and what should be different at the end? → session goal.
- A short breathing exercise to arrive.
- Name: what were you called as a child? The name appears in the text later.

### Phase 2 – Understanding (psychoeducation)

- What the inner child is, in simple words; shadow child and sun child; adult
  self; protectors. Expandable deeper reading for the curious.
- The seven needs the evaluation is later calculated from.

### Phase 3 – Resources (before the hard part!)

- Safe place (guided imagery, details are noted and brought back later).
- Inner helper figure (a real or invented compassionate figure).
- Sun child: what did you love as a child, what were you good at, who was good
  to you?

### Phase 4 – Tracing (history)

- Mood at home, how sadness, anger, mistakes, joy and going your own way were
  handled.
- Roles in the family, sentences heard often.
- Formative experiences (voluntary, with "I'd rather not answer").
- Today: trigger situations, the feeling in them, what you then do.

Every answer option carries weights on needs, core beliefs and protective
strategies. The evaluation is traceable — as in LinuxKompass, you can expand
which answer contributed to what.

### Phase 5 – Mirroring

The site summarises like a therapist who listens: "What I hear …" — carefully
worded, with "Does that fit?". It shows the needs that went shortest, the most
likely core beliefs and the protective strategies. The person picks 1–3 core
beliefs that really feel true and rates how true (0–100 %).

### Break point

After the mirroring, the "Understanding" part ends. Whoever is tired or wanted
to split the session saves here on the device and continues on another day. The
step into the encounter takes strength; nobody should go into imagery exhausted.

### Phase 6 – Encounter (imagery)

Three ways in:

- **Affect bridge** (Encounter/Depth): a situation from today that is moderately
  distressing (not the worst!), feel the feeling in the body, drift back to an
  early moment with the same feeling.
- **Childhood photo**: look at a real photo of yourself as a child.
- **At the safe place** (Gentle, and available to everyone): the child comes to
  your safe place, you first see it from a distance.

Guided sentence by sentence, at your own pace, read aloud on request. Then: how
old is the child? What does it look like? What does it feel? And: **How do you
feel towards it?** — compassion, curiosity, warmth → continue. Impatience,
rejection, shame, emptiness → a responsive text of its own (IFS: this part may
step back a little; that is not a mistake but information).

### Phase 7 – Re-nurturing (rescripting)

- Who steps in? Your adult self, the helper figure, or both.
- What does the child need now? (protection, comfort, being seen, permission to
  feel, relief from guilt, play, someone who sets limits, getting out of the
  situation.)
- A guided scene depending on the need.
- The sentences: suggested from needs and core beliefs, own ones can be added.
  They are said to the child in the imagery.
- How does the child react? (comes closer, cries, smiles, distrusts, turns away,
  nothing) → responsive text.
- Take the child along to the safe place.
- Rate distress and core beliefs again.

### Phase 8 – Protectors (Depth only)

- Address your own main strategy (perfectionism, people-pleasing, withdrawal …)
  as a protector: what do you protect against? Since when? What does it cost?
- Thanks, and an offer: "I, as an adult, will take care now too. You may rest a
  little." Not abolition — relief.

### Phase 9 – New sentences

- For each chosen core belief a realistic new sentence (suggestions plus your
  own wording), believability 0–100 %.
- Depth: collect evidence (three experiences that speak for the new sentence).

### Phase 10 – Letters

- Letter to the child: a draft is assembled from the session (name, age, image,
  needs, sentences), freely editable.
- Depth: the child's reply — with the non-dominant hand on paper, instructions
  on the page, optionally typed up.

### Phase 11 – Into everyday life

- A daily 3-minute ritual (also as its own page, available any time).
- An if-then plan for your own most frequent trigger situation.
- Needs list: concrete small actions per need that went short, to choose from.
- Playtime: one thing for this week from the sun child answers.
- A 7-day plan.

### Phase 12 – Closing

- Vault exercise: whatever still weighs is locked away safely.
- Back to the safe place, then consciously back into the room (5-4-3-2-1).
- Wellbeing and distress again → before/after comparison.
- "What do you take with you?" and an acknowledgement of your own work.
- Aftercare: what can happen in the next days; when professional help makes
  sense — with concrete routes (116 117, consultation hours, cost
  reimbursement).

### Result: session record

One page with everything that came out of it — printable as PDF, downloadable as
a text file. Saved on the device on request for follow-up sessions.

## 5. Personalisation — how the site "listens"

- **Seven needs**: safety, closeness & comfort, being seen & accepted, being
  allowed to show feelings, autonomy & confidence, play & lightness, orientation
  & limits (schema therapy + Grawe).
- **15 core beliefs**, each with: wording, how it feels, where it typically comes
  from, sentences the child would have needed to hear, realistic new sentences,
  related needs.
- **12 protective strategies**, each with: what it does, what it protects
  against, what it costs, a sentence of thanks, an offer of relief, an alternative.
- **Weights**: every tracing answer distributes points. The evaluation shows the
  top entries with reasons ("because you said …").
- **Responsive texts** at five points in the imagery.
- **The child's name** and **age** are inserted into the texts.

## 6. Safety — not negotiable

1. **Opening conversation with a safety question.** Acute suicidal thoughts →
   crisis page, no memory work.
2. **Pause button on every session page.** Opens an emergency kit: breathing,
   5-4-3-2-1, feeling your feet, cold water, naming your surroundings. Then three
   ways on: continue, continue more gently (switch to "Gentle", memory work is
   skipped), end carefully (jump to the closing).
3. **Titration.** Explicitly choose a *moderately* distressing situation
   (3–6 out of 10), never the worst.
4. **Distress measurement** before and after the imagery. Above 7 before the
   encounter → offer to stabilise instead of going deeper.
5. **Distancing techniques** on the gentle path (child through a window, on a
   screen, at the safe place).
6. **Every session ends stabilised**: vault, safe place, reorientation — also
   after "end carefully".
7. **Clear limits** on the start, help and about pages: no substitute for
   therapy, when professional care is needed, crisis numbers for DE, AT, CH and
   international.

## 7. Privacy

- No servers, no analytics, no cookies, no external fonts.
- Answers live in `sessionStorage` (they survive a reload and disappear with the
  tab). Permanent storage on the device only after an explicit choice, deletable
  at any time.
- **Unlike LinuxKompass, no answers in the address bar** — these are intimate
  contents that do not belong in history, bookmarks or shared links.
- Read-aloud uses the browser's speech output; depending on the browser this may
  go through the vendor. The note is next to the button.

## 9. Technology

Like LinuxKompass: Vite + React + TypeScript, no further dependencies, hash
router, German and English, light and dark theme, keyboard- and screen-reader-
friendly, prints cleanly, GitHub Pages via GitHub Actions. Tests (Vitest) check:
both languages present everywhere, all links between answers, needs, core
beliefs and protective strategies valid, the evaluation gives expected results,
every path ends with the closing.
